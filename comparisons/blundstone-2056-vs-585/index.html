Blundstone 2056 vs 585: Which Should You Buy?

The #2056 and #585 use the same Rustic Brown waxed nubuck and receive almost identical fit guidance. The decision comes down to the ground beneath you: pay extra for the 2056’s Vibram outsole, or choose the simpler 585 for everyday streets and casual wear.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 28 August 2026
Quick answer

Buy the 2056 for mixed ground; buy the 585 for normal everyday wear

Choose the Blundstone 2056 if dog walks, gravel, wet parks, country tracks or variable surfaces are a routine part of your week. Its rubber Vibram outsole is designed for more purposeful traction, cushioning and durability. Choose the Blundstone 585 if most of your time is spent on pavements, in shops, at work or travelling. It retains the relaxed, lined comfort and Rustic Brown finish without charging for an outsole you may not need.

Neither boot is fully waterproof, neither replaces a technical hiking boot and neither offers lace-adjusted ankle lockdown. The 2056 is the more capable crossover Chelsea boot; the 585 is the cleaner value choice for lifestyle use.

What the 2056 and 585 have in common

These are closer above the sole than the series names suggest. Both are unisex pull-on Chelsea boots in Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, with elastic sides, front and rear pull tabs, removable insoles and a lined, relaxed fit. Blundstone describes both as true to size and says they should have a worn-in feel sooner than its firmer Originals.

The shared leather is important. Waxed nubuck has a mottled surface that varies naturally between pairs and develops individual character with wear. If you like the colour and casual texture of the 585 but need more ground-focused traction, the 2056 preserves much of that visual character. If you dislike natural shade variation, neither is the ideal choice.

Both are also water-resistant, not waterproof. Marketing language around outdoor use should not be read as evidence of a waterproof membrane or sealed Chelsea-boot opening.

Vibram vs Classic sole: the difference that matters

Blundstone 2056: terrain-focused Vibram rubber

The #2056’s defining component is its rubber Vibram outsole with PU midsole. Blundstone highlights slip resistance, cushioning, traction, transverse flex points and a tread designed to shed mud, snow and stones. It also states resistance to oil, water and acid, heat resistance up to 300°C and cold-environment suitability down to −15°C.

Those are material and design claims, not a guarantee against slipping and not evidence of safety certification. Surface condition, gait and contamination still matter. The practical conclusion is narrower: the 2056 is better specified when a normal week includes more than clean, dry pavement.

Blundstone 585: lifestyle-focused TPU outsole

The #585 uses Blundstone’s Classic sole construction: a TPU outsole, PU midsole and SPS shock-protection system, paired with a contoured EVA footbed containing Poron XRD® at the heel. It is designed around everyday shock absorption and durable general use rather than an aggressive trail-style tread.

That does not make the 585 a poor walking boot for ordinary life. It makes it the more economical choice when commuting, shopping, sightseeing and dry park paths dominate your use. Paying for the 2056 only makes sense if its sole answers a recurring need.

Fit, sizing and break-in

Official fit guidance is effectively a tie. Both models are described as true to size, relaxed from the start and quicker to form to the foot than a firm Original-series boot. Different soles and individual leather variation can still change how two pairs feel, so identical size labels do not guarantee an identical underfoot experience.

Blundstone uses UK sizing. Check length before width: leave roughly 1 cm between your longest toe and the front, and expect only slight heel lift in this pull-on construction. Half sizes add width and volume rather than length. If a UK 8 is long enough but tight across the forefoot or instep, compare an 8.5. If it is too short, move to the next full size.

Do not size around a future stretch: the leather may ease slightly over the instep, but it will not stretch longer. Test the boots indoors with your normal socks before committing.

Which is better for walking?

For mixed-surface everyday walking, the 2056 is the stronger choice on specification. The Vibram outsole, deeper tread and rubber compound give it a clearer advantage for gravel, damp parks, compacted paths and routes that move repeatedly between town and easy countryside.

For urban mileage, travel and firm, predictable surfaces, the 585 remains a sensible option. Its removable XRD-equipped footbed and shock-protection construction are aimed at everyday comfort, and the less terrain-focused sole is not a meaningful disadvantage if rough ground is rare.

Neither model provides the adjustable heel hold, ankle structure or waterproof system of a dedicated hiking boot. For steep, muddy or technical routes, use purpose-built footwear. Our guide to walking in Blundstones explains that boundary in more detail.

Is the 2056 more waterproof than the 585?

No. Both official UK pages describe water-resistant leather, and both boots have elastic panels and an open ankle where water can enter. The 2056’s outsole may give more confidence on variable wet ground, but it does not seal the upper or create a waterproof membrane.

Either model should cope with normal British drizzle, damp pavement and brief showers when the leather is maintained. Prolonged rain, standing water and long wet grass can exceed that protection. If keeping the foot dry is non-negotiable, choose a genuinely waterproof model rather than assuming All-Terrain means all-weather.

Appearance, care and long-term ownership

The 585 has the more familiar Classic profile, so it slips easily into casual work, travel and weekend wardrobes. The 2056 looks more substantial below the upper because the Vibram tread is visibly more rugged. Choose according to the clothes and surfaces you actually encounter rather than treating one silhouette as universally better.

Care is almost identical because both uppers are Rustic Brown waxed nubuck. Remove loose dirt, let damp boots dry naturally at room temperature and use products designed for rustic leather. Blundstone recommends non-pigmented Rustic Renovating Cream and a suitable waterproof spray after testing a hidden area. Avoid radiators and smooth-leather polish that can alter the finish.

The sole units are directly moulded rather than traditional welted constructions intended for straightforward resoling. Lifespan depends on frequency, rotation, surfaces, moisture and care; specifications alone cannot promise a fixed number of years.

Is the Blundstone 2056 worth more than the 585?

At the official UK prices checked on 28 August 2026, the 2056 cost £210 and the 585 £185—a £25 difference. That is a relatively focused premium: both share the same colour family and broadly similar fit, so most of the extra value sits in the Vibram sole and its mixed-surface capability.

  • Traction matters more than a cleaner Classic profile.
  • You want one boot to bridge town and easy country use.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Blundstone 2056 or 585?

Choose the 2056 for its terrain-focused Vibram rubber outsole and the 585 for straightforward urban and casual use at a lower official UK price. They share Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, a relaxed lined fit and water-resistant rather than waterproof uppers.

Do the Blundstone 2056 and 585 fit the same?

Their official guidance is very similar: both are described as true to size, relaxed from the first wear and available in half sizes that add width rather than length. The different sole units can still make the boots feel different underfoot.

Is the Blundstone 2056 more waterproof than the 585?

No. Both use water-resistant Rustic Brown waxed nubuck and neither official UK product page describes the boot as fully waterproof. The 2056 outsole improves traction; it does not add a waterproof membrane.

Is the Blundstone 2056 worth the extra money over the 585?

The premium is worthwhile when you regularly walk on gravel, park paths, mud or other mixed surfaces where the Vibram outsole solves a real need. For mainly dry pavements and casual wear, the 585 is usually the better-value choice.
