Blundstone 1306 vs 1609: Dress or Classic?

A sharper Dress silhouette or a rounded Classic? Compare the fit, leather and buying trade-offs of the 1306 Rustic Brown and 1609 Antique Brown before choosing your pair.

By BootsGuide Editorial3 November 2026

Choose the shape and fit first

Choose the 1306 if you want a chisel-toe profile and Rustic Brown finish, and its Dress fit works for your feet. Choose the 1609 if you prefer a rounded Chelsea outline and Antique Brown leather on the Classics platform.

These are two different routes to a brown pull-on boot. A familiar size or the word “brown” does not make them interchangeable. Our recommendation is to settle the fit and silhouette before deciding whether a small price difference matters.

Chisel toe or rounded Chelsea?

Look at the outline with the trousers you normally wear. The 1306’s elongated front gives it a more tailored appearance; the rounded 1609 keeps more of the familiar casual Chelsea shape. That is a styling distinction, not evidence that one boot is appropriate for every office or occasion.

Our practical shortlist is the 1306 for someone who specifically wants that sharper toe, and the 1609 for someone who likes a rounder boot but wants a smooth brown finish. If you dislike the silhouette in the photographs, a discount is unlikely to change how often you reach for it.

Do not transfer your size automatically

The official 1306 page describes a roomier, longer fit and suggests trying a smaller option, particularly on narrow feet. The 1609 page gives true-to-size advice for a relaxed fit. Those starting points are different.

There is also a distinction between Blundstone’s general half-size guidance and a model-specific sizing recommendation: half sizes add width rather than length. Do not interpret “half a size down” as a precise reduction in internal boot length. Our UK size guide explains the labels and fit families.

  1. Try both boots with the socks you actually plan to wear.
  2. Check toe clearance while standing and walking; do not trade usable length for a tighter ankle.
  3. Assess the instep and heel together. A foot that slides forward needs reassessment, not just thicker socks.
  4. Compare neighbouring options indoors under the seller’s return terms if the first size is uncertain.

Our advice is to choose the model that works on both feet without an elaborate workaround. If neither option gives you a comfortable starting fit, keep looking rather than treating break-in as a remedy for the wrong shape.

Rustic Brown and Antique Brown need different care

The 1306’s waxed-nubuck finish has a more varied surface; the 1609 is specified as waxed full-grain leather. Brown colour alone is not enough to choose a conditioner or polish. Check the leather description before buying care products.

Blundstone’s care guide separates smooth full-grain leather from coated nubuck. It recommends material-compatible treatment, natural drying and a discreet test before wider application. For the 1306’s Rustic finish, use the coated/waxed-nubuck instructions rather than the instructions for plain nubuck.

Our buying suggestion is to pick the finish you want to maintain, not the one that looks easiest in a thumbnail. Keep a record of the model and material with your care kit so the two routines do not get mixed up. The Blundstone cleaning guide provides the steps.

Comfort, walking and weather limits

Both models have cushioning features, but a materials list cannot tell us how a particular foot will feel after a full day. We have not worn these pairs side by side, timed their break-in or measured grip. There is no comfort score or long-distance winner attached to this article.

Our suggestion is to make the indoor fit decision first, then introduce longer wear gradually. For travel or a busy workday, keep a familiar pair available during that process. Read walking all day in Blundstones if distance is the main reason for buying.

For wet weather, neither the toe shape nor a darker finish establishes better protection. Check each product’s current weather specification and our rain-protection guide. A casual Chelsea should not be chosen as a substitute for purpose-built hiking or safety footwear when those are the actual requirements.

UK price and availability

On 3 November 2026, the official UK reference prices were £185 for the 1306 and £195 for the 1609. Both UK product listings reported available size variants at that check. These are dated manufacturer references, not Amazon prices or a promise that your size will remain in stock.

A £10 difference is a poor reason to accept the wrong fit. Our recommendation is to pay attention to the version, usable size and returns before treating one listing as better value. The more useful choice is the pair you will wear comfortably and want to keep.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between Blundstone 1306 and 1609?

The 1306 combines a chisel-toe Dress shape with Rustic Brown waxed nubuck. The 1609 uses a rounded Classics shape and Antique Brown waxed full-grain leather. Choose fit and silhouette before comparing the price.

Do the 1306 and 1609 fit the same?

Do not assume they do. The official 1306 advice describes a longer, wider fit; the 1609 is described as true to size with a relaxed Classics fit. Try the exact models rather than copying one size between them.

Should I size down in the 1306?

The UK 1306 page suggests considering a half or full size down, particularly for narrow feet. Treat this as a fitting option, not a rule for everyone, and check length, instep hold and heel movement indoors.

Which looks smarter?

Our styling preference is the 1306 when a sharper chisel-toe outline matters. The 1609 offers a rounded Chelsea shape with a smoother Antique Brown finish. Either choice should work with the clothes you already wear.

Which is better for long walks?

This comparison does not establish a walking-comfort winner. Fit, surfaces and the wearer matter. Use the specifications to shortlist, then assess comfort gradually; we have not run a distance or wear test.

Does the higher 1609 price mean it will last longer?

No lifespan advantage is established here. The extra cost should be considered alongside the construction, fit and finish you want, not as a promise of greater durability.
