Blundstone 566 vs 585: Do You Need Thermal Boots?

A warmer boot is only an upgrade when it suits your routine. Compare the black 566 Thermal with the Rustic Brown 585 Classic before choosing your next Chelsea boots.

By BootsGuide EditorialPrepared and source-checked 20 October 2026

Choose for your coldest regular day, not an imagined expedition

Our recommendation is the 566 when cold outdoor time is a recurring part of your week. Choose the 585 when most wear involves ordinary errands, commuting and indoor destinations, and you want a brown everyday boot. Extra warmth can be useful without making the Thermal the better choice for everyone.

Fit: account for socks and the footbed

The official UK size guide explains that half sizes add width and space over the foot rather than length. It recommends roughly 1 cm of toe room and notes that slight heel lift can be normal. Materials and model shape still affect the fit.

Our fitting advice is to try both boots indoors with your intended socks. A pair chosen using thin everyday socks may feel different when you add your winter socks. Walk, turn and stop; check for toe contact, painful pressure across the instep and excessive heel movement.

Do not size up automatically because the label says Thermal. Equally, do not squeeze into a smaller pair simply to eliminate every trace of heel lift. Assess the whole movement while you can still return the boots. An insole change also changes the available room, so recheck fit after any adjustment.

When the Thermal option earns its place

Think about where you spend your time, not just the outdoor temperature. Waiting at a bus stop, watching an outdoor match and walking briskly to a heated office are different buying scenarios. Our preference would be the 566 for repeated cold outdoor waits; for short journeys followed by hours indoors, we would start by trying the 585.

That is a recommendation based on intended use, not a measured temperature limit. We cannot say how many degrees warmer one will feel, or promise that either will keep every wearer comfortable throughout winter. Socks, activity and individual sensitivity matter.

If you already have an everyday pair that works, a dedicated seasonal pair may fill a clearer gap than replacing it with something warmer for every occasion. If this is your only boot purchase, consider the mild days as carefully as the cold ones.

Black Thermal or Rustic Brown Classic?

Our styling choice is the black 566 with darker denim and a winter wardrobe built around black, charcoal or navy. We would choose the 585 with blue jeans, olive trousers and textured casual layers. These are outfit suggestions, not rules.

Keep colour separate from function. If you prefer brown but genuinely need a thermal boot, do not choose the 585 just because it is brown; explore the appropriate thermal model separately. Likewise, wanting black does not by itself mean you need insulation.

Weather protection is not an ice-grip guarantee

The UK 566 page describes treated water-resistant leather, coated elastic and sealed elastic seams; its fit section also calls the boots waterproof. The 585 is described as water-resistant. We would shortlist the 566 for the stronger weather-focused construction, while checking the exact retailer specification.

We have not tested leakage or immersion, and neither reference establishes a safe-on-ice guarantee. Insulation and traction solve different problems. For icy routes, deep water or a specific workplace requirement, select footwear against that requirement rather than relying on the word Thermal.

For more context, read our guide to Blundstone water protection. Avoid turning a manufacturer's weather claim into a promise about every surface or condition.

Treat each finish as its own care decision

Choose care products for the exact leather, rather than applying the same treatment to both simply because they share a brand. Check the current manufacturer instructions and test an unfamiliar treatment discreetly; appearance changes can matter particularly when you bought a boot for its finish.

Our ownership advice is to include time for cleaning and drying in the purchase decision. If you expect to wear one pair every day in poor conditions, consider how you will give it time between wears. A more expensive boot does not remove that practical constraint.

Is the extra cost worth it?

The official reference-price gap is £25 at this check. Our view is that it is worth considering when the Thermal addresses a recurring problem in your routine. It is less persuasive if warmth is only an occasional concern and most use is indoors or in mild conditions.

Compare delivered prices in your actual size, seller details and return terms. A low headline price may apply to another colour or size. We have not verified live Amazon stock for every option and do not claim that the linked listings are the cheapest available.

Choose the 566 for a clear cold-season role; choose the 585 for your preferred everyday use and finish. If neither fits securely, the right decision is to try another shape rather than forcing a choice between these two.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Thermal boots for a UK winter?

Not automatically. Consider your regular outdoor time, activity and indoor use. Our shortlist favours the 566 for repeated cold outdoor periods and the 585 for varied everyday wear.

Can thick socks turn the 585 into a 566?

Treat socks as a fit and comfort choice, not as proof that two different constructions perform identically. Check that your intended socks leave enough room.

Should I buy a larger Thermal size?

Do not size up automatically. Try the exact pair with your intended socks and footbed, checking toe room, instep pressure and heel movement.

Is the 566 guaranteed to grip on ice?

No ice-grip guarantee is established by the references used here. Thermal protection does not establish traction on an icy surface.

Were the boots tested side by side?

No. This comparison uses manufacturer specifications and editorial buying advice, without measured wear-test results.
