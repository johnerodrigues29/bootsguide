Blundstone 500 vs 510: What's the Difference?

The Blundstone 500 and 510 share the same Original-series construction, price and fit guidance. The buying decision is mainly visual: Stout Brown with contrasting details or a cleaner Voltan Black finish.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 18 September 2026
Quick answer

Choose by colour: their core specifications match

Buy the Blundstone 500 if you want the traditional Stout Brown Original, visible brown elastic and contrasting stitching that work naturally with denim, khaki and earth tones. Buy the Blundstone 510 if you prefer a black-on-black boot for dark denim, grey trousers, uniforms and understated everyday outfits.

There is no published comfort or performance advantage between them. Blundstone lists both as unlined Originals with 2.5 mm water-resistant full-grain leather, a removable contoured EVA footbed, Original TPU sole and the same true-to-size guidance. Both were £175 on the official UK site when checked, so current availability and the colour you will wear most should decide.

What the 500 and 510 have in common

These are colour siblings, not different performance tiers. Both belong to the Originals series and use the direct construction most closely associated with the classic Blundstone shape. The upper is a single-piece, unlined full-grain leather design, which keeps the boots relatively simple and breathable but gives them a firmer first feel than the lined Classics.

Underfoot, both use a removable contoured EVA footbed and Blundstone's Original sole unit. The official product pages describe a durable TPU outsole, an ergonomically engineered toe spring and XRD® technology in the heel as part of the SPS MAX comfort system. Their reinforced heel construction, elastic sides and front and rear pull tabs also match.

Blundstone says the outsoles resist acid, organic fat and oil, but neither listing presents these lifestyle models as certified safety footwear. If your workplace requires protective toe caps, tested slip resistance or a particular safety standard, select a suitable Safety-series boot that meets the employer's rules rather than assuming the 500 or 510 qualifies.

The real difference: Stout Brown vs Voltan Black

Blundstone 500: Stout Brown

The 500 is the heritage-looking choice. Stout Brown is a dark, smooth leather rather than a light tan, and the brown elastic, lighter stitching and warmer pull-tab details make its colour more visible than the name alone suggests. It pairs easily with blue denim, olive, beige, corduroy and brown accessories.

Because the colour is deep, the 500 can look almost charcoal in subdued light, then show warmer brown tones outdoors. That makes it versatile, but buyers wanting an unmistakably medium-brown boot may find it darker than expected. Check the current retailer photography and model number rather than relying on a generic “brown” filter.

Blundstone 510: Voltan Black

The 510 uses a more uniform black treatment across the leather, elastic and sole. It is easier to combine with black jeans, grey trousers, dark workwear and wardrobes where brown footwear would introduce an unwanted contrast. The monochrome appearance also makes the rounded Original shape look slightly cleaner.

That does not turn it into a formal dress boot. The broad toe, pull tabs, visible elastic and practical TPU sole retain the casual, workwear-inspired character. Choose the 510 for colour versatility and a quieter finish, not because it is built to a smarter or more technical specification.

Fit and sizing: the same starting point

Blundstone describes both models as true to size, with a snug fit across the top and toe area when new. The thicker leather should soften and mould around the foot with wear, but it will not stretch in length. Toes touching the front are therefore a size problem, not something to solve through break-in.

Blundstone half sizes add width and volume rather than extra length. If the boot is long enough but too tight over the foot, the corresponding half size is the logical option. If it is too short, move up a full size. Aim for roughly 1 cm of space ahead of the longest toe, test with your normal socks and expect a small amount of heel lift in this pull-on design.

The published guidance gives no reason to size the 510 differently from the 500. Minor differences between physical pairs and individual leather panels are still possible, so try both when convenient and judge pressure at the instep, toe room and heel friction. Our Blundstone size guide explains the complete check.

Comfort and break-in: another tie

Neither model has the leather-and-textile lining, extra footbeds or upgraded removable footbed found in the Classics. Their appeal is the simpler Original construction: a firm leather upper that gradually forms to the foot, a removable EVA footbed and heel-focused shock absorption.

Expect the flex point and instep to ease through ordinary wear rather than dramatic stretching. Start with short indoor sessions on a clean surface, then increase walking time as long as there is no sharp pressure, numbness or worsening heel rub. Heat, soaking and forced stretching can damage leather without correcting inadequate length.

For everyday commuting, travel and firm paths, either model can be comfortable once correctly fitted. For a more relaxed feel from the first wear, compare the lined 585 Rustic Brown or 587 Rustic Black. Our break-in guide separates normal firmness from warning signs.

Weather resistance: colour changes the look, not the claim

Both boots use 2.5 mm water-resistant full-grain leather. They are reasonable for ordinary UK showers, damp pavements and brief wet-ground contact when correctly maintained, but neither is fully waterproof. The elastic panels and ankle opening remain entry points, and prolonged saturation exceeds the stated protection.

Black may disguise some rain marks, while Stout Brown can make gradual patina more visible, but that is an appearance difference rather than a technical advantage. Avoid deep puddles and extended wet terrain. If the boots become wet, remove the footbeds and let everything dry naturally at room temperature, away from radiators and hairdryers.

For genuinely cold and wet winter use, the 566 Thermal adds insulation and weather-focused construction. Our guide to Blundstone water resistance explains why ordinary leather treatment does not make an open-ankle Chelsea boot equivalent to a waterproof hiking boot.

Care and ageing

The same smooth full-grain care approach applies to both models. Remove loose dirt with a soft brush or lightly damp cloth, air dry, then use a suitable leather cream, polish or waterproofing product after testing it on a hidden area. Avoid saturating the leather or elastic and remove excess product from seams.

The 500 will usually show a warmer, more obvious patina as creases and small marks develop across the Stout Brown surface. The 510's black finish can look more uniform from a distance, though pale dust, salt marks and dry patches may stand out. Appropriate black cream can refresh colour when needed, while neutral products reduce the risk of over-darkening the 500.

Neither colour has a published durability advantage. Rotation, gait, surfaces, moisture, fit and maintenance will affect lifespan far more than the pigment. Read our material-specific cleaning guide before applying a new product.

Alternatives worth considering

If you like the brown palette but want a relaxed, lined fit, the Blundstone 500 vs 585 comparison explains the step from Originals to Classics. If black is essential but the 510 feels too firm or uniform, compare it with the Rustic Black Blundstone 587, which adds lining, a more relaxed fit and an XRD-equipped footbed.

The smooth Walnut Brown Blundstone 550 is another useful middle ground: it keeps full-grain leather but moves to the comfort-focused Classics platform. These alternatives change construction as well as colour, so they are more meaningful choices than paying extra for a different finish on the same Original specification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Blundstone 500 and 510?

The main published difference is colour: the 500 is Stout Brown and the 510 is Voltan Black. Both are unlined Originals with 2.5 mm water-resistant full-grain leather, a removable contoured EVA footbed and the Original TPU sole.

Do Blundstone 500 and 510 fit the same?

Yes. Blundstone gives both models the same true-to-size guidance and describes them as snug across the top and toe when new. Half sizes add width rather than length.

Are Blundstone 500 and 510 waterproof?

No. Both use water-resistant full-grain leather but are not fully waterproof. They suit ordinary showers when maintained, not prolonged saturation or deep water.

Is the Blundstone 510 more formal than the 500?

The 510's black-on-black appearance is easier to pair with dark and smart-casual clothing, but both remain rounded-toe pull-on workwear-inspired Chelsea boots rather than formal dress boots.

Should I buy the Blundstone 500 or 510?

Choose the 500 for Stout Brown, warmer outfits and more visible contrasting details. Choose the 510 for a uniform black finish and dark neutral wardrobes. At the same price, colour and availability should decide.
