Blundstone 2056 vs 500: Is the All-Terrain Upgrade Worth It?

Is a Vibram sole worth paying extra for if you mainly wear Chelsea boots around town? Compare the 2056 with the original 500 by fit, finish and the surfaces you actually use.

By BootsGuide EditorialSource-checked 27 October 2026
Quick answer

Choose the upgrade for a specific need, not just the All-Terrain name

The 2056 is the more compelling shortlist option if you want a lined boot and a terrain-focused outsole. The 500 is a sensible starting point for everyday casual use when its fit suits you and you prefer to spend less. Neither choice guarantees better comfort for every foot.

Our recommendation is based on manufacturer specifications, not a side-by-side wear test. Before paying extra, decide whether your priority is the sole, the leather finish or the fit: this comparison changes all three, unlike a simple colour comparison.

What does the Vibram upgrade buy you?

The 2056 changes the outsole package rather than simply adding a different badge. Blundstone positions its All-Terrain series around traction and durability. That makes it relevant when mixed surfaces are part of your routine, but the specification is not a measured grip score against the 500.

Think about a typical week. A route combining pavements and maintained park paths gives you a reason to consider the upgrade. If nearly every journey is between the car, an office and shops, there may be less reason to prioritise the outsole over fit and price.

Do not interpret the name as a guarantee on wet tiles, algae, mud or ice. Surface conditions, tread wear and your footing still matter. We have not measured comparative slip resistance or outsole lifespan, so cannot promise a percentage improvement or extra years of service.

Fit: try both, even if you already own Blundstones

The UK product guidance describes the 2056 as relaxed and the 500 as snug across the top and toe area. That is useful starting guidance, not a guarantee of how either will feel on your foot. Lining and upper construction are reasons to check both rather than assuming the same size produces an identical fit.

Blundstone half sizes add width and space over the foot, not length. If your toes meet the front, a half size is not the solution to a length problem. Use the UK sizing guide before choosing an unfamiliar label.

  • Try your intended socks and check both feet.
  • Stand, walk and bend naturally indoors while preserving return eligibility.
  • Check toe clearance and pressure over the instep.
  • Reject persistent rubbing or painful compression instead of relying on break-in to solve it.

A cheaper pair that fits comfortably can be better value than a higher-specification pair that you avoid wearing. Likewise, a higher price is reasonable if the actual fit and features address your needs.

Rustic Brown or Stout Brown?

This is also a finish decision. Compare the photographs with the exact colour selected at checkout; retailer listings sometimes combine several models and colours. Natural leather variation means your pair may not match a studio image exactly.

Choose the appearance you want to maintain. Do not assume one polish is appropriate for every brown leather: follow the maker’s instructions for the particular finish, and test any treatment discreetly first. For a closer look at each model, see the 2056 review and 500 review.

Water resistance is not a waterproof guarantee

Both product pages describe water-resistant leather. Neither specification here is a reason to plan prolonged immersion or assume waterproof construction. The Vibram outsole does not, by itself, establish how much water can enter through the upper or openings.

If staying dry through sustained rain is your main requirement, verify an explicit waterproof specification for the footwear you buy. If your route involves demanding hiking terrain, assess purpose-built footwear separately rather than choosing solely between these two Chelsea boots. When is the extra £35 worth considering?

Shortlist the 2056 when…

  • The outsole is a deliberate priority.
  • You prefer its lined construction and rustic finish.
  • Your trial fit feels right without compromise.
  • The actual checkout premium fits your budget.

Shortlist the 500 when…

  • You want a straightforward everyday Chelsea boot.
  • You prefer its finish and unlined construction.
  • Its fit suits your feet and socks.
  • You would rather keep the difference for another purchase.

Compare like-for-like sizes, seller reliability and returns. A low headline price on the wrong variant is not a useful saving. Check whether a return requires paying postage, and whether the selected size is actually available before treating a listing as an offer.

If you already own a comfortable 500, the 2056 is not automatically a necessary replacement. Identify the problem you want to solve first. If the existing pair meets your needs, buying a second pair purely for the label may add little practical value.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 2056 just a 500 with a different sole?

No. The official specifications also differ in finish and lining. Compare the complete boot, not only the outsole.

Do I need a different UK size?

Try both with your usual socks. Product fit guidance differs, and Blundstone half sizes add width rather than length.

Is the 2056 waterproof?

Its UK listing describes water-resistant leather. Do not treat the All-Terrain label as a waterproof guarantee.

Have you tested these boots side by side?

No. This comparison combines official specifications with editorial buying advice, without personal wear-test claims.
