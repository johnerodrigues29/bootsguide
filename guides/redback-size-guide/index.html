Redback Size Guide: UK and Australian Sizing Explained

Redback boots are sold in UK sizes that are also the Australian sizes on the pull tab. This guide explains the conversions, what half sizes really change, women’s and unisex fit, and how to check a pair indoors before you commit.

By BootsGuide EditorialSource-checked 29 October 2026
The short answer

Start with your usual UK size, and treat half sizes as width

Redback sizing is UK/Australian, so your usual UK size is the sensible starting point. Redback’s own guidance says a half size changes mainly the width and a whole size changes the length. The boots are also described as generously proportioned to allow thick socks, so judge a doubtful size indoors with the socks you will actually wear, rather than guessing from a conversion alone.

Redback’s US sizing page gives the same rule in words: a UK size reads one size smaller than the US men’s size, so a US men’s 10 is a UK 9. It says boots display the Australian size on the pull tab and that all sizes are an approximation. Half sizes are not available in every model or retailer; one UK retailer lists UBOK half sizes from 4.5 to 11.5, so check the selector for your own size.

Half sizes, width and thick socks

According to Redback’s US guidance, going up a half size changes mainly the width, while going up a whole size changes the length rather than the width. A trade size chart puts the difference at about 5 mm of extra width per half size, with no extra length. That means a 9.5 is a wider 9, not a shorter 10.

The same guidance says the boots are made to accommodate thick work socks, and suggests a separate leather insole (sold separately) with thick socks if a pair feels loose at first. Width descriptions differ between sources: a UK distributor lists a standard G width, while Redback’s US page describes fitting B through EEE. For that reason we do not give a letter width; use the generous-fit idea and judge the pair on your own foot.

Our view is that a half size is worth considering only when the length is already right and the boot feels narrow. One UK retailer advises that unless you have particularly wide feet it is usually best to stick to whole sizes, which matches that logic.

Women’s and unisex sizing

Redback styles such as the UBOK are sold in unisex sizes. Redback’s US page says women’s sizes can read about 2½ sizes smaller than US women’s sizes, so a US women’s 10.5 is a UK 8. A UK retailer adds that Redbacks can be quite roomy for women and suggests dropping down a size if you are borderline.

We treat that retailer note as a prompt to compare rather than a rule. Measure your foot, compare it with the foot-length column, and check both the nearer size and the one below it indoors. If you normally buy UK women’s sizes, do not assume the number matches a Redback number; use the measurement and the fit checks instead.

A practical measurement and try-on routine

Our suggested buying routine is to measure first, compare listings second, and make the final decision with both boots on.

  1. On a level floor, place paper against a wall and stand with your heel lightly against it.
  2. Mark the end of your longest toe, measure heel to mark, and repeat for the other foot. Keep the larger measurement.
  3. Find that figure in the foot-length column, then note the UK size beside it and the size either side.
  4. Try the boots indoors on a clean surface with the thick or boot socks you intend to wear. Stand, walk, turn and sit.
  5. Check that the foot enters and leaves without forcing, and that the size on the pull tab matches the size you ordered.

Keep the packaging and confirm return conditions before a longer try-on. Do not wear a doubtful pair outdoors simply to see whether it improves.

When to change size

Toes press the front or the boot feels shortCompare the next whole size; a half size will not add length.
Length feels right but the foot feels squeezed from the sidesCompare the next half size, which adds width without length.
The foot slides forward or the heel lifts badly with thick socksRecheck the socks and consider an insole before moving a whole size down.
One foot feels right and the other does notAssess each foot separately and size for the larger one.

These are screening prompts, not a guarantee of comfort. Stop a try-on that causes pain or numbness, and do not rely on break-in to lengthen a boot that is too short.

Frequently asked questions

Are Redback boots sized in UK or US sizes?

Redback boots are made in UK/Australian sizing. Redback’s US page says a UK size reads one size smaller than the US men’s size, so a US men’s 10 is a UK 9. Always check the size label the seller is displaying.

Do Redback half sizes add length?

No. Redback guidance says a half size changes mainly the width, and a trade size chart gives about 5 mm of extra width per half size. A whole size is the step that changes length.

What size should women order in Redback boots?

Redback sizes are unisex. Redback’s US page says women’s sizes can read about 2.5 sizes smaller, and a UK retailer says the boots can feel roomy for women and suggests dropping down when borderline. Compare your measured foot length with the chart and check the fit indoors.

Do Redback boots suit wide feet?

They are described as generously proportioned to allow thick socks. Sources describe the width differently, so we do not give a letter width. If the length is right but the boot feels narrow, compare the next half size, which adds width without length.

Have you personally tested these sizes?

No. This is a source-based guide with an editorial fitting checklist, not a personal wear test.
