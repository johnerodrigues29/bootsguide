Sizing & fit guide

Blundstone Size Guide

The detail that catches buyers out: Blundstone half sizes add width, not length. Here is how to turn that rule into a practical fit check.

By BootsGuide editorialUpdated 15 August 20266 min read

The quick answer

Start with your usual UK size. If you need extra width or room over the instep, a half size may help; if you need extra length, move up a full size. Confirm the result standing up in your normal socks.

Source note: Fit guidance was checked against the official Blundstone UK size guide and official UK FAQ on 15 August 2026.

How Blundstone sizing works

Blundstone marks its UK sizing on the sole. Model materials and shapes can still affect feel, so the stamped number is a starting point rather than a guarantee across every series.

Half sizes explained

A UK 7.5 is not simply a longer UK 7. The brand’s guidance says the half size increases space over the foot to create a wider fit. This is particularly relevant for people with a higher instep or who feel pressure across the top of the foot.

Do not use a half size to solve toes touching the front. That points to a length problem, so compare the next full size.

How much space should there be?

The manufacturer suggests roughly 1 cm between your longest toe and the front of the boot. Your toes should not touch the front when standing. A small amount of heel lift can be normal in a pull-on Chelsea boot, but the boot should not feel uncontrolled.

Do not size for hoped-for stretch

Leather can ease slightly over the instep with wear, but it should not be expected to stretch in length. Start with adequate toe room.

Series fit matters

The Classics — including the 550 and 585 — are described by the brand as more relaxed across the top and toe, with an additional footbed option to personalise volume. Originals are described as firmer over the top and toe when new. Other series use different lasts and linings, so check the individual model page rather than applying one rule to the entire range.

A five-step home try-on

  1. Use your real boot socks. Thin dress socks can give the wrong impression if you will usually wear thicker pairs.
  2. Try both feet standing. Most people have some difference between feet; fit the larger one.
  3. Check toe clearance. Make sure your longest toe is not touching the front.
  4. Walk on carpet. Notice instep pressure, side pressure and whether heel movement settles into a controlled rhythm.
  5. Do not mark the soles. Keep the boots returnable until you are confident.

Wide feet

Start with the half-size rule, then compare series. A more relaxed Classics shape may be a useful candidate, but foot width, toe shape and instep height are different variables. A retailer try-on remains the best check when your feet are particularly wide or different from each other.

Common sizing mistakes

  • Using a half size for extra length.
  • Judging fit while seated rather than standing.
  • Trying boots with socks much thinner than the intended pair.
  • Expecting the upper to stretch lengthwise.
  • Keeping a visibly worn pair while still deciding.

Common questions

FAQ

Do Blundstones run big or small?

The official guidance says many styles run true to size, but series shape and materials change the feel. Use the individual model fit notes.

What does a Blundstone half size mean?

It adds width and volume over the foot rather than length.

Is heel lift normal?

A slight amount can be normal in a pull-on boot. Excessive movement or loss of control suggests the volume may not be right.

Will Blundstones stretch?

The upper can ease slightly over the instep, but the manufacturer says the boots do not stretch lengthwise.
