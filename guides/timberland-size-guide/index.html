Timberland Size Guide: Finding Your UK Fit

Identify the adult model, use the right UK chart and assess both boots indoors. Our reference is the men’s Premium 6-Inch in Yellow; other Timberland ranges need their own checks.

By BootsGuide Editorial30 October 2026
The short answer

Choose the exact boot before choosing the size

Start with the product code, the correct UK size chart and measurements of both feet. Then assess the shortlisted size indoors with your usual boot socks. “Timberland 6-Inch” covers more than one product, so a size recommendation for a different range, lining or age group is not enough.

Our reference is the men’s Premium 6-Inch in Yellow, TB110061713, listed on Timberland’s UK site. We have not established a universal instruction to go half a size down, and we have not tested this boot on foot.

Using the official Timberland size chart

Open the exact UK product page and choose Size Chart, or visit Timberland’s official size charts. Match the chart to the product’s men’s, women’s or children’s category before reading the UK, EU or US columns.

Record both your heel-to-toe measurement and the UK size suggested by the relevant chart. A foot-length value and an internal boot-length value are different measurements; read the chart’s heading rather than assuming they are interchangeable. Do not apply a men’s US conversion to a women’s or Junior listing.

Measure both feet, then try both boots

The following is our editorial shopping routine. It helps you organise a fitting; it is not a Timberland guarantee of comfort or a substitute for the product’s current chart.

  1. On a flat floor, stand on paper with your heel lightly against a wall. Mark the end of your longest toe and measure from the wall to the mark.
  2. Repeat for the other foot and keep both measurements. Use the larger foot when assessing whether the boot has enough length.
  3. Note which socks you intend to wear. If you normally use an orthotic, bring it to the fitting and check the boot’s available volume with it in place.
  4. Compare your measurement with the exact product’s chart. Treat your usual trainer size as a reference rather than proof of the correct boot size.
  5. Try both boots on a clean indoor surface. Lace them evenly, walk, turn and stop; assess toe clearance, pressure across the foot and heel movement together.

Keep the packaging and check return conditions before trying multiple sizes. Make the decision before outdoor wear or treatments that could affect a return.

If you are between sizes or the fit feels wrong

Compare the neighbouring options under the seller’s return terms rather than committing to a smaller size because of a general internet rule. Assess length and overall volume separately: tightening laces cannot create more toe space, and a longer boot is not automatically a better answer to width pressure.

Stop a fitting that causes pain or numbness. We do not specify a universal amount of heel lift or toe clearance because we have not measured this model on different feet. A comfortable indoor fitting is the useful decision point, not a promised break-in result.

Frequently asked questions

Should I go half a size down in Timberlands?

Do not apply that rule to every Timberland boot. Check the advice for the exact adult model, compare measurements with its official chart and assess both boots indoors. We did not verify a model-specific size-down recommendation for TB110061713 in this check.

Are half sizes available in the UK Premium 6-Inch?

The official UK Yellow TB110061713 page listed several half sizes on 30 October 2026, including UK 6.5, 7.5 and 8.5. That does not establish stock for every colour, size or retailer.

Is a UK size the same as a US size?

No. Use the correct regional column and the men’s, women’s or children’s chart for the selected product. Do not reuse one conversion across all ranges.

Will break-in fix boots that are too short?

Do not buy on that assumption. If your toes repeatedly touch the front or the boots cause pain during an indoor fitting, reconsider the size or shape before wearing them outdoors.

Has BootsGuide tested these boots on foot?

No. This guide uses manufacturer product information and an editorial fitting checklist. It does not report a wear test or measured internal boot dimensions.
