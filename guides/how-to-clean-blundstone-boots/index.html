How to Clean Blundstone Boots Without Damage

The safest cleaning method depends on the upper. A cream that revives smooth leather can permanently change nubuck or suede, so identify the material before you reach for any product.

By BootsGuide EditorialFact-checked and updated 21 August 2026

Safe default

Brush, wipe, air dry—then treat the correct material

Start with the least aggressive method: remove loose dirt, use minimal moisture and let the boots dry naturally. Only then apply a material-compatible cream, spray or protector after testing a small hidden area.

2. Cleaning full-grain leather

  1. Remove caked dirt with a soft-bristled brush or lightly damp cloth.
  2. Let the boot air dry away from heaters and direct sunlight.
  3. Test a suitable leather polish, spray or cream on a hidden area.
  4. Apply a small amount with a cloth or brush, using circular motions.
  5. Wipe away residue and let the boot dry again.

Use less product than you think. Repeated heavy layers can alter colour and leave residue around seams and elastic.

3. Cleaning waxed nubuck and Rustic leather

Blundstone lists the 585 and 587 among its waxed-nubuck Rustic styles. Remove dirt with a damp cloth and allow the boot to dry. The brand recommends its non-pigmented Rustic Renovating Cream and waterproof spray for coated waxed or oiled nubuck.

  1. Wipe surface dirt gently—do not scrub one patch aggressively.
  2. Air dry completely.
  3. Test the rustic cream or spray where a colour change will not be obvious.
  4. Apply evenly according to the product instructions.
Important: the Rustic cream is for coated nubuck finishes. Blundstone says it should not be used on plain nubuck.

4. Cleaning plain nubuck and suede

Plain nubuck

Use a dry nubuck cloth to lift surface dirt, then brush gently in small circular motions. A gum eraser can help with scuffs. If using a nubuck cleaner, follow its instructions and avoid concentrating on one area for too long.

Suede and waxed suede

Use a suede brush for surface dirt and a gum eraser for marks. For deeper cleaning, lightly dampen the brush with water and a small amount of suitable suede-and-leather cleaner, dab excess moisture with a clean towel and air dry. Restore the nap gently after drying.

5. Soles, elastic and footbeds

Use a soft brush and damp cloth around the sole edge, taking care not to flood the join. Wipe elastic panels gently rather than saturating them. Remove insoles and hand-wash them with mild soap and water only when necessary, then rinse and dry flat naturally.

Drying and storage

  • Air dry at room temperature with the footbeds removed.
  • Never use a radiator, hairdryer or other artificial heat source.
  • Store in a dry, airy place away from heat and damp.
  • Let recently worn boots dry before putting them in a closed cupboard.
  • Reapply water protection approximately every three months depending on use and weather.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use normal shoe polish on Blundstones?

Only when it suits the upper. Smooth full-grain leather can use an appropriate polish or cream, but waxed nubuck, plain nubuck and suede need different products. Always test a hidden area.

How do I clean Blundstone 585 boots?

The 585 uses waxed nubuck. Remove dirt with a damp cloth, let it dry naturally, then use a product intended for rustic waxed nubuck, such as non-pigmented Rustic Renovating Cream and suitable waterproof spray.

Can Blundstones go in a washing machine?

No. Hand-clean the upper using the material-specific method. Removable insoles can be gently hand-washed and dried flat away from heat.

Can I dry Blundstones on a radiator?

No. Blundstone recommends natural air drying away from direct sunlight and heaters because concentrated heat can damage and dry the leather.
