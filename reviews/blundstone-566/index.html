Blundstone 566 Review: Warmth, Fit & Value

The #566 is Blundstone’s black Thermal Chelsea boot: insulated, weather-focused and made for buyers who want winter protection without moving to a bulky lace-up boot. This review explains when its warmth earns the premium—and when a year-round Classic makes more sense.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 1 September 2026
Quick verdict

A convincing winter Chelsea boot—if you will use the insulation

The Blundstone 566 is the model to shortlist for cold commutes, winter travel, wet school runs and everyday outdoor routines where ordinary leather Chelsea boots feel under-specified. Thinsulate insulation, a genuine sheepswool-lined footbed and enhanced protection around the leather, elastic and seams make it meaningfully different from the standard Classics. It remains easy to pull on and smart enough for casual daily wear.

The trade-off is seasonality. The same construction that keeps cold out can feel unnecessarily warm indoors or during mild weather, and the ankle opening still prevents the coverage of a taller waterproof boot. At the official UK price of £210 when checked, the value depends on winter use—not simply on wanting a black Blundstone.

Best for

Cold, wet everyday conditions, winter commuting, travel, outdoor errands and buyers who prefer pull-on convenience.

Skip it if

You need a light year-round boot, technical hiking support or reliable protection through deep water.

This is a winter specification rather than a cosmetic variation. The combination of treated upper, coated elastic, seam treatment, insulation and fleece-lined footbed changes both protection and fit compared with a normal uninsulated Classic.

How warm is the Blundstone 566?

Warmth is the #566’s clearest reason to exist. Thinsulate sits in the lining to reduce heat loss without turning the boot into an oversized snow style, while the sheepswool surface underfoot adds insulation where cold can travel through the sole. Blundstone markets the Thermal range for warm, dry winter use rather than as an ordinary fashion boot.

That design should suit low-temperature everyday routines: waiting on platforms, walking the dog, standing at weekend sport or spending a winter day moving between streets and outdoor venues. Warm socks can still matter, but the boot itself contributes more insulation than the unlined #500 or standard lined #585.

There is no single temperature rating that guarantees comfort for every buyer. Circulation, activity level, sock choice, moisture and time outdoors all change how warm feet feel. If most of your day is spent in heated offices, shops or public transport, the 566 may become too warm. A standard Classic plus season-appropriate socks gives more flexibility across the year.

Is the Blundstone 566 waterproof?

Blundstone calls the #566 weatherproof and specifies water-resistant treated full-grain leather, tight-knit coated elastic and sealed elastic seams. That is a notably stronger wet-weather package than the basic water-resistant leather used on many Chelsea boots. It makes the 566 a logical option for rain, damp pavements, slushy streets and brief contact with wet ground.

It is still important to respect the boot’s shape. The low ankle opening can admit water from above, and no Chelsea boot should be treated like a tall wellington. Avoid deep puddles, flood water and prolonged saturation. The official product page uses both “water-resistant” for the leather specification and “weatherproof” for the finished boot; we would not translate that into an unlimited waterproof guarantee.

Keep the upper conditioned and correctly protected because factory treatment does not remove the need for care. Our guide to Blundstone waterproofing and rain use explains the practical limits in more detail.

Fit, sizing and break-in

Blundstone UK describes the 566 as true to size with the relaxed character of the Thermal Series. However, the sheepswool footbed occupies more volume than a thin standard insole, so the first impression can be snugger than the word “relaxed” suggests. The fleece compresses over time, but it should not be used to justify cramped toes or painful pressure.

Prioritise length. Aim for about 1 cm between the longest toe and the front of the boot. A little heel lift is normal in pull-on footwear, especially before the leather settles, but toes should not touch the front while walking. Blundstone half sizes add width and instep volume rather than length. If a UK 8 is long enough but tight across the foot, compare an 8.5; if it is too short, move to a UK 9.

Try the boots standing, on carpet and with the socks you expect to wear in winter. Buyers planning thick socks should assess the complete system rather than ordering from a trainer size alone. The full-grain leather can soften across the instep, but it will not stretch lengthwise. See our complete Blundstone size guide before ordering.

Comfort for walking and long days

The #566 uses Blundstone’s Classic sole unit: a durable TPU outsole over a PU midsole with the brand’s SPS shock protection. Blundstone says the system absorbs up to 90% of walking impact, while the contoured EVA footbed provides the familiar base and adds a sheepswool surface. As a specification, it is well suited to everyday mileage on pavements, firm paths and routine errands.

The boot is not a technical hiking design. Elastic sides cannot tighten around the foot like laces, the ankle is low and the outsole is not the terrain-focused Vibram unit fitted to the Blundstone 2056 All-Terrain. Choose the 566 for cold-weather daily walking; choose a supportive waterproof hiking boot for steep, technical or prolonged wet routes.

The removable footbed is useful for drying and replacement. An orthotic may fit, but changing the thick sheepswool footbed can alter both warmth and internal volume, so test the combination carefully.

Leather, construction and everyday style

The smooth black full-grain upper makes the 566 quieter visually than rustic nubuck models. It works with dark denim, workwear trousers and casual winter outfits, while the round toe and pull tabs retain the recognisable Blundstone profile. The coated black elastic blends into the upper rather than creating a contrast panel.

The practical winter details are discreet. You do not see the Thinsulate from outside, and the sheepswool is mainly visible around the footbed. That makes the boot easier to wear beyond overtly outdoor settings than many insulated snow boots. It is still a substantial casual Chelsea boot, not a narrow formal style.

Double stitching, durable leather and the TPU outsole support a long-use design, but lifespan depends on rotation, surface wear, moisture and care. BootsGuide does not assign a fixed number of years based on specifications alone.

Care and durability

Remove loose dirt with a soft brush or damp cloth and let wet boots dry naturally at room temperature. Do not place leather next to a radiator or use strong direct heat: rapid drying can harden the upper and damage materials. Remove the sheepswool footbeds to air them separately when damp.

Use a conditioner and weather-protection product that is suitable for smooth black full-grain leather, testing it on a hidden area first. A pigmented black renovating cream may restore colour, while treatment frequency should follow use and exposure rather than a rigid calendar. Keep the elastic and seam areas clean without saturating them.

Salt and road grime deserve prompt attention in winter because residues can mark leather. Our Blundstone cleaning guide covers material-safe steps and drying.

Is the Blundstone 566 worth it?

At £210 on the official UK site when checked, the #566 asks a meaningful premium over ordinary lifestyle Chelsea boots. The price is easiest to justify when it can replace both a daily casual boot and a separate mild-winter option. Buyers who commute on foot, spend weekends outdoors or regularly face cold rain will use its insulation and weather-focused construction.

It is poor value if the boots will live mainly in heated indoor settings or be worn throughout mild months. In that case, a 585 or 550 offers more seasonal range, and warm socks can cover occasional cold days. It is also poor value as a substitute for specialist footwear when you genuinely need tall waterproof coverage, technical grip or workplace certification.

Frequently asked questions

Are Blundstone 566 boots waterproof?

Blundstone describes the 566 as weatherproof and specifies water-resistant treated leather, coated elastic and sealed elastic seams. This is stronger wet-weather protection than a standard Chelsea boot, but the ankle opening means it is not the same as a tall waterproof boot for deep water or prolonged saturation.

Are Blundstone 566 boots warm?

Yes. The 566 combines Thinsulate insulation with a removable genuine sheepswool-lined footbed, making it one of Blundstone’s most winter-focused Chelsea boots.

Do Blundstone 566 boots run true to size?

Blundstone UK describes the 566 as true to size with a relaxed Thermal-series fit. The sheepswool footbed makes it feel snugger at first and compresses with wear. A half size adds width rather than length.

What is the difference between Blundstone 566 and 585?

The 566 is a black Thermal model with Thinsulate, a sheepswool footbed and enhanced weather protection. The 585 is a Rustic Brown Classic without the same winter insulation, making it more versatile across seasons.
