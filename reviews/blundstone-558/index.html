Blundstone 558 Review: Fit, Comfort & Value

The #558 is the smooth black member of Blundstone’s Classics family: leather lined, more relaxed than the Original #510 and equipped with a removable XRD® comfort footbed. It is the logical black everyday option for buyers who value fit flexibility over the lowest price.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 22 September 2026
Quick verdict

A more comfortable route to a classic black Blundstone

Choose the Blundstone 558 if you want the clean appearance of a smooth black Chelsea boot but prefer the roomier, lined construction of the Classics series. Its strongest argument is not a dramatic technical upgrade: it is the combination of a relaxed fit, leather-and-textile lining, an XRD® heel insert and two-footbed volume adjustment in a versatile black upper.

The main alternative is the Blundstone 510. It gives you a similar smooth-black look for less money, but on the firmer, unlined Originals platform. If both fit comfortably, the decision comes down to whether the 558’s lining and underfoot package justify the premium.

Best for

Everyday urban wear, commuting, travel and buyers who want a relaxed black Classic.

Consider first

It is water-resistant rather than waterproof, and the pull-on design cannot be tightened with laces.

Key strengths

  • Relaxed Classics fit
  • Smooth black full-grain leather
  • Leather-and-textile lining
  • Removable XRD® footbed

Trade-offs

  • Costs more than the 510
  • No lace-based lockdown
  • Not a technical hiking boot
  • Needs full-grain leather care

The specification places the 558 squarely alongside the 550 and 585. The important difference is the upper: smooth black full-grain leather rather than Walnut Brown full-grain leather or Rustic Brown waxed nubuck.

How does the Blundstone 558 fit?

Blundstone describes the 558 as true to size and the Classics family as more relaxed across the top and toe area than the Originals. That makes it a sensible starting point for buyers with a higher instep or anyone who found the 500 or 510 too firm over the forefoot. It does not mean the boot will suit every wide foot: heel shape, instep height and sock thickness still matter.

A correctly sized pair should leave roughly 1 cm between the longest toe and the front of the boot. Slight heel movement is normal in a pull-on Chelsea boot, particularly before the upper settles. Toes touching the front, painful pressure across the instep or uncontrolled heel lift are signs to revisit the size rather than wait for a dramatic transformation.

Important half-size rule: Blundstone half sizes add width and volume, not length. Move up a full size if you need additional toe length; use the half size when the length is right but the boot feels too tight across the foot.

The Classics also include an additional pair of footbeds. This gives you a practical way to reduce internal volume if your heel feels loose without shortening the boot. Try the boots indoors on carpet with your usual socks before deciding which footbed setup works best.

Comfort and break-in

The 558’s comfort proposition has three parts: a more relaxed last, a leather-and-textile lining and the contoured EVA footbed with Poron XRD® technology at the heel. Blundstone says the model has a relaxed feel from the outset and takes less time to form to the foot than its unlined Originals. Those are manufacturer fit claims, not a promise that every buyer will experience zero break-in.

Smooth full-grain leather can still feel structured during the first outings. Start with shorter periods, wear the socks you intend to use long term and let the leather soften naturally. The upper may ease across the instep, but it will not become longer. Avoid soaking the boot, applying heat or wearing painful pressure points in the hope of forcing a fit.

Underfoot comfort is likely to suit pavements, commuting and ordinary day-long use better than technical terrain. The sole system prioritises cushioning and everyday durability, while the pull-on upper prioritises convenience. For long, steep or uneven routes, a laced walking boot gives more precise heel and ankle control.

Black full-grain leather: appearance and care

The #558 uses smooth black full-grain leather, giving it a cleaner and slightly smarter appearance than the mottled finish of the Rustic Black 587. It works with dark denim, workwear and smart-casual clothing without looking as rugged as waxed nubuck. Black also makes routine colour matching simple, although dust, dried salt and surface scratches may be more visible.

Care should follow the full-grain leather method. Remove dry or caked dirt with a soft brush or damp cloth, allow the boot to air-dry away from heaters and direct sunlight, then apply a compatible polish, spray or cream sparingly. Test any product on a discreet area first because treatments can alter sheen or colour. Wipe away residue and let the boot dry naturally.

Do not use the Rustic Renovating Cream intended for waxed nubuck simply because other Classics models use it. The 558, 550 and 510 have smooth full-grain uppers and need products designed for that finish. Our Blundstone cleaning guide explains the material-specific routines.

Blundstone 558 vs 510, 550 and 585

558 vs 510

These are the two closest choices for someone who wants a smooth black Blundstone. The 510 is an unlined Original with a firmer fit over the top and toe area. The 558 is a lined Classic with a more relaxed shape, XRD-equipped footbed and an extra footbed for volume adjustment. Choose the 510 for the simpler construction and lower price; choose the 558 when its fit and comfort package are worth the premium.

558 vs 550

The 558 and 550 share the same Classics platform and smooth full-grain leather specification. Colour is the decisive difference: the 558 is black, while the 550 is Walnut Brown. Fit, lining, footbed and sole claims are effectively aligned, so wardrobe preference should make the decision.

558 vs 585

Both are lined Classics, but the 585 uses Rustic Brown waxed nubuck. Pick the 558 for a cleaner black finish that can look smarter; pick the 585 for a deliberately varied, rugged surface that develops a different patina. Their care products are not interchangeable.

Is the Blundstone 558 waterproof?

No. Blundstone describes the upper as water-resistant, not waterproof. Maintained full-grain leather should cope with routine wet pavements, splashes and light rain, but the elastic side panels and Chelsea construction are not designed for extended saturation. Water can eventually enter around the elastic or top opening, particularly in deep puddles or prolonged heavy rain.

For normal British day-to-day conditions, sensible care and drying are more important than expecting membrane-level protection. Remove surface moisture after use, let the boots dry at room temperature and restore the leather treatment when it begins to look dry. For repeated winter exposure or genuinely wet work, the Thermal 566 provides a more weather-focused package. See our guide to Blundstone water resistance for the limits of each series.

Is the Blundstone 558 good for walking?

The 558 is best viewed as an everyday walking and standing boot. Its cushioning, flexible pull-on format and relaxed fit suit commuting, travel and mixed indoor-outdoor routines when the shape matches your foot. The TPU outsole provides useful everyday traction, but it is not the deep, terrain-focused outsole found on a hiking boot.

If your routes include mud, loose ground or steeper trails, the All-Terrain 2056 adds a Vibram outsole and a more ground-focused design. The 558 remains the better-looking choice for urban use and smart-casual outfits. Our guide to walking in Blundstones covers the distinction in more detail.

Is the Blundstone 558 worth £185?

At the official UK price checked on 22 September 2026, the 558 sits above the Original 510. The extra spend buys a lined Classics construction, more relaxed fit, XRD-equipped removable footbed and a second footbed for volume adjustment. Those benefits have real value when they produce a better fit or make the boot more comfortable for your routine.

If the 510 already fits you well and you prefer a firmer, less padded feel, the 558 premium is harder to justify. If you need the Classics shape, want smooth black rather than rustic leather and expect to wear the boot frequently, the 558 is one of the most coherent options in the range.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Blundstone 558 and 510?

Both are black full-grain leather Chelsea boots. The 558 is a lined Classic with a more relaxed fit and a Poron XRD-equipped footbed, while the 510 is an unlined Original with a firmer fit and simpler footbed.

Does the Blundstone 558 run true to size?

Blundstone states that the 558 runs true to UK size. A half size adds width and volume rather than length, while a full size is needed for extra length.

Is the Blundstone 558 waterproof?

The 558 uses water-resistant full-grain leather but is not a fully waterproof boot. It suits routine wet pavements and light rain when cared for, not prolonged saturation.

Is the Blundstone 558 suitable for wide feet?

The Classics series has a more relaxed fit across the top and toe area than the Originals. A half size provides additional width, but individual instep and heel shape still determine the correct fit.

Is the Blundstone 558 worth buying?

The 558 makes most sense for buyers who want black smooth leather with the lining, adjustable-volume footbeds and relaxed fit of the Classics series. The cheaper 510 is the stronger value choice if a firmer, unlined Original fits well.
