Blundstone 500 Review: Fit, Break-In & Value

The #500 is the foundation of Blundstone’s Original series: an unlined Stout Brown Chelsea boot with a firmer new-boot feel and fewer comfort extras than the Classics. That simplicity is exactly why some buyers prefer it.

By BootsGuide Editorial Updated and fact-checked 28 October 2026
Quick verdict

The simpler, firmer Original

Our buying recommendation is to shortlist the 500 if you want the iconic round-toe Original in smooth Stout Brown leather and prefer an unlined boot. Expect a firmer feel than the lined Classics, and judge it on a careful indoor try-on rather than a promise of instant comfort. We have not personally wear-tested this pair or measured cushioning, grip or lifespan.

Best for

Everyday casual wear, buyers who prefer an unlined boot and those wanting the core Original design.

Skip it if

You want a relaxed fit immediately, a lined boot, full waterproofing or technical trail support.

Key strengths

  • Classic Stout Brown finish
  • Unlined for simpler feel
  • 2.5 mm water-resistant leather
  • Removable contoured EVA footbed

Trade-offs

  • Firmer initial fit
  • More wear-in than Classics
  • Fewer comfort extras than Classics
  • Not fully waterproof

Two details are worth reading carefully. The upper is crafted around a single piece of leather and is unlined by design, which Blundstone links to breathability. Underfoot, XRD® technology appears in the 500’s heel as part of the SPS MAX Comfort system, so compare the full specification of each model rather than the XRD name alone.

Fit and break-in

Blundstone says the Originals have a firm fit across the top and toe area and often run true to size. With repeated use the thick leather softens and moulds to the foot and may ease slightly over the instep, but it will not become longer. The official size guide recommends roughly 1 cm of space beyond your toes and notes that a slight amount of heel lift is normal when walking.

If the length is right but the forefoot feels too narrow, compare the corresponding half size, because Blundstone half sizes add width, not length. If the boot is short, a half size will not fix it; consider the next full size.

An indoor fitting checklist

  1. Try both boots standing, with the socks you intend to wear, on a carpeted or clean surface that preserves return eligibility.
  2. Check for about 1 cm in front of the toes. If the front of the foot touches the boot, the pair is too short.
  3. Walk and turn before judging. Firm over the instep is expected; sharp pressure, numbness or toes gripping to keep the boot on is not a good sign.
  4. Recheck if you change sock thickness or add an insole, because extra padding reduces useful space.
Break-in rule: stiffness that gradually eases is different from toes hitting the front, sharp pressure or numbness. Blundstone says unlined Originals need a few extra wears for the leather to soften, but no fixed period is promised, so do not use break-in as a reason to keep the wrong size.

Comfort compared with the Classics

The 500 pairs a removable contoured EVA footbed with Blundstone’s SPS MAX Comfort system, which the official page describes as an ergonomic toe spring combined with XRD® technology in the heel. What it does not have is the leather-and-textile lining of the Classics. Blundstone says the lined Classics feel comfortable from the first wear, while unlined Originals take a few extra wears to soften. The 550 and 585 also use a footbed described with Poron XRD® technology, so buyers who prioritise a softer first wear or a roomier instep should compare the Classics before deciding.

The unlined build may appeal to buyers who prefer less bulk and a more direct leather feel. Comfort remains foot-specific: we cannot promise an all-day result, and the simpler model is not automatically less comfortable once properly fitted.

Rain, winter and walking

The official UK page describes 2.5 mm water-resistant leather. That is not a waterproof membrane boot, and the elastic panels and opening limit protection in deep water or standing water. Our recommendation is to assess the 500 as everyday footwear for changeable weather with the right care, not as a guaranteed dry-foot boot. Let wet boots dry naturally and never speed the process with a radiator. See our Blundstone waterproofing guide before buying mainly for wet conditions.

We would consider the 500 for commuting, errands and easy paths if it passes an indoor fit check. That is a buying suggestion, not a measured walking test. For slippery mud, long wet routes or uneven ground, compare the All-Terrain #2056 or a dedicated walking boot, and read our walking and hiking guide.

Durability and care

Blundstone’s care guide for full-grain leather starts with a soft-bristled brush or damp cloth to remove caked-on dirt, followed by air drying away from direct sunlight and heaters. It then suggests a leather polish, spray or cream, tested first on an inconspicuous area in case it changes the colour. Its Polishing Pad is described as an easy-to-apply conditioner for when full-grain boots look scuffed.

The same guide warns that salt, milk, solvents and strong acids or alkalis can damage leather and recommends cleaning boots straight away after exposure. Store them somewhere cool and ventilated. Blundstone also states that lifespan varies with frequency, surface, exposure and care; its general two-to-seven-year estimate is not a guarantee. Read our cleaning guide and lifespan guide for the wider routine.

Frequently asked questions

Do Blundstone 500 boots run true to size?

Blundstone says the Originals often run true to size, with a firm fit across the top and toe. Half sizes add width rather than length, so consider one only when the length is already right. Check for about 1 cm of toe room while standing.

Do Blundstone 500 boots need breaking in?

Blundstone says the thick unlined leather softens and moulds to the foot with wear, but no fixed period is promised. Break-in will not lengthen a boot that is too short, and persistent rubbing or toe pressure is a reason to reassess the size.

Are Blundstone 500 boots waterproof?

The official UK page describes 2.5 mm water-resistant leather, not a waterproof boot. Treat it as an option for everyday changeable weather with care, not for prolonged wet exposure or standing water.

What is the difference between the Blundstone 500 and 585?

The 500 is an unlined Original with a firmer initial fit. The 585 is a lined Classic with waxed nubuck, a more relaxed fit and a footbed described with Poron XRD technology. Both list SPS shock-protection technology, so compare the exact specifications rather than one feature name.

Is the Blundstone 500 good for walking?

It is an option to try for everyday streets and easy paths if the fit passes an indoor check. BootsGuide has not performed a walking test or measured grip or fatigue. For slippery or uneven ground, compare the All-Terrain 2056.
