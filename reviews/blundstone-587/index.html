Blundstone 587 Review: Fit, Comfort & Value

The #587 puts the relaxed comfort of Blundstone’s Classics series into a versatile Rustic Black Chelsea boot. This review explains its fit, waxed-nubuck finish, rain limits and whether it is a better buy than the near-identical Rustic Brown #585.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 8 September 2026
Quick verdict

The everyday black Blundstone for buyers who prefer a rustic finish

The Blundstone 587 is a strong all-round choice for commuting, travel, casual work settings and everyday walking. It shares the lined, relaxed Classics construction of the popular 585 but changes the colour from Rustic Brown to Rustic Black. That gives buyers the same comfort-focused platform with an easier match for dark denim, black trousers and understated wardrobes.

Its main limitation is easy to miss: black colour does not make it a winter or waterproof model. The waxed nubuck is water-resistant, but the elastic sides and ankle opening still limit wet-weather protection. At the official UK price of £185 when checked, the 587 makes sense when its exact combination of relaxed fit and mottled black leather will be worn often.

Best for

Everyday wear, commuting, travel and buyers wanting a comfortable black Chelsea boot with visible character.

The specification is the core Classic formula rather than a performance variation. Buyers are paying for a lined, comfort-focused everyday boot and the Rustic Black upper—not insulation, a waterproof membrane or the All-Terrain Vibram outsole.

Rustic Black waxed nubuck: what the finish is really like

Rustic Black is not a uniform, highly polished black. The waxed-nubuck surface has muted variation and a slightly weathered appearance, allowing creases and small marks to become part of the boot rather than standing out immediately. It looks more casual than smooth full-grain leather and works especially well with denim, workwear-inspired layers and relaxed winter clothing.

Blundstone keeps its leathers close to their natural state and warns that shade and grain can vary from product images. Two pairs can therefore show slightly different mottling before wear. That is normal for this finish, not automatically a quality defect. Buyers wanting the cleanest, most consistent black should compare a smooth-leather model such as the #510 Original.

The 587’s upper uses the same care family as Rustic Brown #585 and Rustic Brown #2056. Ordinary black shoe polish can flatten the texture or change the finish, so colour should not be used as the only clue when selecting care products.

Comfort and underfoot feel

The 587 combines a leather-and-textile lining, contoured EVA footbed, Poron XRD® cushioning in the heel and a PU midsole. Blundstone describes its SPS MAX system as absorbing up to 90% of high-speed impact energy. That manufacturer claim supports the model’s everyday comfort positioning, although personal comfort still depends on correct fit, gait and surface.

The removable footbed gives buyers some control over internal volume and makes replacement easier. Classics are also commonly supplied with an extra footbed to help personalise fit; check the current retailer package rather than assuming every listing includes the same accessories. A compatible orthotic may work after removing the standard footbed, but it can change heel height and instep pressure.

For city streets, commuting, airport days and ordinary errands, the 587 is one of the easier Blundstones to shortlist. The trade-off is that a moulded sole unit is not the traditional stitched and readily resoleable construction some heritage-boot buyers expect.

Fit, sizing and break-in

Blundstone UK says the 587 runs true to size, has a relaxed fit from the start and takes less time to form to the foot than firmer models. “True to size” should be treated as a starting point, not a substitute for checking the boot on your own feet.

Length comes first. Aim for roughly 1 cm between the longest toe and the front of the boot. Slight heel lift is normal in a pull-on Chelsea boot because there are no laces to lock the heel, but toes striking the front or repeated rubbing are warning signs. Blundstone half sizes add width and volume rather than length. If a UK 8 is long enough but tight across the forefoot, compare an 8.5; if it is too short, move to a UK 9.

Try the boots standing, on carpet and with your intended socks. The waxed nubuck will soften and ease slightly over the instep but will not stretch lengthwise. Do not keep a painful pair on the assumption that the toe box will become longer. Our Blundstone size guide explains half sizes, heel lift and series differences in detail.

Are Blundstone 587 boots waterproof?

No. The official UK specification describes the waxed-nubuck upper as water-resistant. The 587 should cope with ordinary showers, damp pavements and brief contact with wet ground when the leather is correctly maintained, but it is not designed for prolonged saturation, deep puddles or standing water.

The elastic panels and ankle opening are practical entry points for moisture, while treatment wears with use. If cold and wet winter conditions are the priority, the Blundstone 566 Thermal adds treated leather, coated elastic, sealed elastic seams, Thinsulate insulation and a sheepswool footbed. For sustained wet routes, use a purpose-built waterproof boot with suitable coverage.

Read our guide to Blundstone waterproofing and rain use before interpreting “water-resistant” as an unlimited promise.

Is the Blundstone 587 good for walking?

The 587 is well suited to everyday walking when the fit is correct. Its cushioning, flexible pull-on construction and familiar Classic sole make sense for commuting, sightseeing, shopping and firm park paths. The black finish is also easy to integrate into travel wardrobes where one pair must cover several casual settings.

It is not a technical hiking boot. The elastic upper cannot provide the adjustable heel lockdown of laces, the ankle is low and the tread is less terrain-focused than the Vibram sole on the Blundstone 2056 All-Terrain. Choose the 587 for everyday distance rather than steep, muddy or heavily loaded routes. Our walking guide covers these limits.

There is no meaningful performance winner. The two models share the construction details that affect fit, comfort and ordinary walking. Choose the 587 if Rustic Black fits your wardrobe; choose the 585 if you prefer the warmer, more recognisable Rustic Brown appearance. Buy on colour and current availability, not the belief that one has a more advanced sole.

Other alternatives worth considering

Choose the Blundstone 550 for the same relaxed Classics family in smooth Walnut Brown full-grain leather. Choose the Blundstone 500 or black #510 if you prefer the firmer, unlined feel of the Originals and a simpler specification. Choose the 566 Thermal for winter insulation or the 2056 All-Terrain when mixed-surface grip matters more than colour.

The 587 is most convincing for buyers who have already decided on a relaxed Classic and want black without a polished look. If the priority is performance in rain, snow or rough ground, start with that requirement before choosing the finish.

Care, colour and durability

Brush or wipe away loose dirt gently, then allow damp boots to dry naturally at room temperature. Avoid radiators, hairdryers and other strong heat sources that can harden or damage leather. Remove the footbeds to air separately if moisture reaches the inside.

For waxed-nubuck Rustic finishes, Blundstone recommends its non-pigmented Rustic Renovating Cream and a suitable waterproof spray after testing on a hidden area. Do not automatically use pigmented black polish: it can darken the boot unevenly and change the characteristic texture. Expect the Rustic Black colour to develop individual variation with wear.

The double stitching and durable TPU outsole are designed for regular use, but no honest review can promise a fixed lifespan from specifications alone. Rotation, surfaces, moisture, gait and care all affect wear. See our Blundstone cleaning guide for material-specific steps.

Is the Blundstone 587 worth it?

At £185 on the official UK site when checked, the 587 is worth considering when it can become a frequently worn everyday boot. The case is strongest for buyers who want the relaxed Classics fit, prefer a rustic rather than polished black and value a removable cushioned footbed.

It is harder to justify if you already own the 585 and only want the same boot in another colour, or if your real need is full waterproofing, insulation or technical grip. In those cases, spend on a genuinely different specification. Because the 587 and 585 are so similar, compare current prices and available sizes before paying more for either colour.

Frequently asked questions

Do Blundstone 587 boots run true to size?

Blundstone UK describes the 587 as true to size with a relaxed Classics fit. Half sizes add width rather than length, so go up a full size if the boot is too short.

Are Blundstone 587 boots waterproof?

No. The 587 uses water-resistant waxed nubuck, which suits normal rain when maintained, but it is not presented as a fully waterproof boot for prolonged saturation or deep water.

What is the difference between Blundstone 587 and 585?

They share the same Classics construction, relaxed fit, lining, XRD footbed and sole unit. The main difference is colour: the 587 is Rustic Black and the 585 is Rustic Brown.

Is the Blundstone 587 good for walking?

It is well specified for everyday urban walking, travel and firm paths. It lacks the laced lockdown, ankle structure and terrain-focused outsole of a technical hiking boot.
