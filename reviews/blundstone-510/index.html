Blundstone 510 Review: Fit, Comfort & Value

The #510 takes Blundstone’s firm, unlined Originals construction and gives it a smooth Voltan Black finish. This review explains the fit, break-in, everyday comfort, rain limits and whether it is the right black Blundstone for your wardrobe.

By BootsGuide Editorial Published 15 September 2026; updated 18 September 2026
Quick verdict

A simple black Original for everyday wear

The Blundstone 510 is a strong choice for buyers who want the recognisable pull-on Original in smooth black leather without the lining and more relaxed volume of the Classics. It is versatile enough for commuting, travel, casual work settings and ordinary daily walking, while the unlined construction keeps the specification direct and familiar.

The key buying question is fit, not colour. Blundstone describes the 510 as true to size but snug across the top and toe when new. Its thicker leather should soften and form to the foot, yet it will not become longer. Buyers wanting a roomier first feel or a cushioned Classics footbed should compare the Rustic Black #587 instead. At the official UK price of £175 when checked, the 510 is most convincing when a smooth black Original is exactly what you want.

Best for

Everyday black footwear, commuting, travel and buyers who prefer the firm, unlined Originals fit.

Skip it if

You need full waterproofing, insulation, technical hiking support or a relaxed fit from the first wear.

The specification is designed for ordinary lifestyle use. Resistance to acid, organic fat and oil is stated on the official product page, but that does not make the #510 a certified safety boot. Buyers who need protective toe caps, slip testing or workplace certification should choose a suitable Safety-series model that meets the employer’s requirements.

Voltan Black full-grain leather

The 510 uses smooth full-grain leather rather than the mottled waxed nubuck found on Rustic models. Its Voltan Black finish looks cleaner and more uniform, pairing easily with black jeans, dark denim, neutral trousers and casual workplace clothing. It is the logical black Original for buyers who do not want the visibly varied surface of Rustic Black.

Smooth does not mean formal. The round toe, thick sole edge, elastic panels and pull tabs keep the 510 firmly in practical Chelsea-boot territory. It can work in relaxed smart-casual settings, but a slimmer dress boot will look sharper with tailoring. Natural leather will also crease and mark with use; regular care can keep the colour even without making the boot look new indefinitely.

The unlined upper is part of the Original formula. It feels more direct against the foot than the leather-and-textile lining in the Classics and can take longer to soften. That simplicity may appeal to buyers who prioritise breathability and a traditional Blundstone feel, while those seeking immediate softness may prefer the #587.

Comfort and underfoot feel

The contoured EVA footbed is removable, which makes airing, replacement and some orthotic swaps easier. Blundstone also describes the sole as using its SPS MAX comfort system, including toe-spring geometry and XRD® technology in the heel to reduce impact and wearer fatigue. Those are manufacturer specifications, not a guarantee that every foot will find the boot comfortable.

Compared with the Classics, the 510 has fewer comfort layers. The #587 and #585 add lining, a more relaxed last and a footbed with Poron XRD cushioning. The 510 therefore suits buyers who prefer a firmer, less padded feel or who already know the Original shape works for them. If comfort over the instep is the priority, do not assume the 510 will quickly turn into a roomy boot.

Footbed changes affect internal volume. A thicker replacement can increase pressure over the top of the foot, while a thinner one may create excessive movement. Check heel position and toe clearance again after any change, and make sure the footbed lies flat without lifting the heel too high.

Fit, sizing and break-in

Blundstone UK says the #510 generally runs true to size, with a snug fit across the top and toe area. The thick leather is expected to soften and mould to the foot over time. That makes the first fit different from the relaxed Classics, but it should still be wearable: sharp pressure, numbness or toes contacting the front are not useful break-in targets.

Check length first. Blundstone recommends roughly 1 cm between the longest toe and the front of the boot. A small amount of heel lift is normal in a pull-on Chelsea boot, but large movement or repeated rubbing may signal too much volume. Try the boots standing, on carpet and with the socks you expect to wear.

Half sizes are often misunderstood. A UK 8.5 is wider than an 8 but is not longer. If the UK 8 has enough toe room yet feels tight across the forefoot or instep, the half size may help. If it is too short, move to the next full size. Leather can ease across flex points, but it will not stretch lengthwise. See our Blundstone size guide and safe break-in guide before committing to a painful pair.

Are Blundstone 510 boots waterproof?

No. The official specification describes 2.5 mm water-resistant leather, not a fully waterproof construction. The 510 should handle ordinary showers, damp pavements and brief contact with wet ground when the leather is maintained, but the elastic panels and ankle opening remain practical entry points for moisture.

A black finish can disguise damp marks better than a lighter colour, but it does not improve the weather rating. Avoid deep puddles, standing water and hours of saturated terrain. Let wet boots dry naturally at room temperature, remove the footbeds to air separately and keep them away from radiators or hairdryers.

For cold, wet winter use, the Blundstone 566 Thermal adds insulation, coated elastic and sealed elastic seams. For long wet routes, use purpose-built waterproof footwear with adequate coverage. Our guide to Blundstone water resistance explains the realistic limits.

Is the Blundstone 510 good for walking?

The 510 is suitable for everyday urban walking, commuting, travel and firm park paths when the shape fits correctly. The EVA footbed, heel cushioning and flexible pull-on design support its everyday role, while black leather makes it easy to use as the only casual boot on a short trip.

It is not a technical hiking boot. The low elastic upper cannot provide lace-adjusted heel lockdown, the sole is not the terrain-focused Vibram unit used by the #2056 All-Terrain, and the water-resistant construction has clear wet-weather limits. Choose it for streets and easy routes rather than steep mud, rocky ground or heavy loads.

Comfort over distance depends on fit and conditioning. A new pair that is still firm across the instep is a poor choice for an all-day walk. Build wear gradually, check for rubbing and use our walking guide to match the boot to the route.

Blundstone 510 vs 587

These are both black Chelsea boots, but they are not the same experience. The 510 is an unlined Original with smooth full-grain leather and a firm initial fit. The 587 is a lined Classic with Rustic Black waxed nubuck, more room over the foot and an upgraded removable footbed.

Choose the 510 if you prefer a uniform black finish, want the simpler Original construction or value its lower official price. Choose the 587 if you want mottled leather, a relaxed fit and more cushioning from the start. Buyers with a high instep should pay particular attention to this distinction rather than choosing only by appearance.

Care and durability

Remove dried dirt with a soft-bristled brush or lightly damp cloth, then allow the boots to air dry away from direct sun and heaters. Once clean and dry, use a conditioner, renovating cream or polish intended for smooth full-grain leather. Test a small hidden area first because care products can change colour and shine.

A black renovating product can help restore colour after scuffs, while a neutral product is less likely to add pigment. Keep creams away from the elastic where possible and avoid heavy layers that leave residue around stitching. If you add a waterproofing product, confirm that it is compatible with smooth leather and follow its instructions.

Reinforced heel stitching and the TPU outsole support regular everyday use, but no specification can promise a fixed lifespan. Frequency, gait, surfaces, moisture, rotation and care all affect wear. Inspect the outsole, elastic, seams and upper rather than relying on age alone. See our cleaning guide and lifespan guide for practical ownership advice.

Is the Blundstone 510 worth it?

At £175 on the official UK site when checked, the 510 offers the central Original design in an especially versatile colour. It is good value when the smooth black finish suits your wardrobe and the firmer unlined fit works for your foot. Its appeal is simplicity, not a long list of winter or hiking features.

It is harder to justify if you expect immediate room over the instep, full waterproofing or insulation. The 587 is the more relevant comfort-focused black alternative, while the 566 Thermal addresses cold weather and the 2056 All-Terrain addresses mixed-surface grip. Buy the 510 because its particular Original construction fits the job, not because black makes it technically more capable.

Frequently asked questions

Do Blundstone 510 boots run true to size?

Blundstone UK describes the 510 as true to size with a snug Originals fit across the top and toe when new. Half sizes add width rather than length.

Are Blundstone 510 boots waterproof?

No. The 510 uses 2.5 mm water-resistant full-grain leather, but it is not a fully waterproof boot for prolonged saturation, deep puddles or standing water.

What is the difference between Blundstone 510 and 500?

Their published UK construction is essentially the same. Both are unlined Originals with full-grain leather, a removable contoured EVA footbed and TPU sole; the main difference is Voltan Black on the 510 versus Stout Brown on the 500.

What is the difference between Blundstone 510 and 587?

The 510 is a firm, unlined Original in smooth black full-grain leather. The 587 is a more relaxed, lined Classic in Rustic Black waxed nubuck with an XRD-equipped footbed.

Is the Blundstone 510 good for walking?

It is suitable for everyday urban walking and easy firm paths when correctly fitted. It is not a technical hiking boot and lacks lace-adjusted hold and terrain-specific support.
