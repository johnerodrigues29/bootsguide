Blundstone 1306 Review: Fit, Comfort & Value

The #1306 combines Rustic Brown waxed nubuck with the longer, sharper profile of Blundstone’s Dress Series. It is a unisex Chelsea boot for buyers who want a smarter silhouette than the round-toe Classics, but its generous length makes careful sizing essential.

By BootsGuide EditorialPublished and fact-checked 29 September 2026
Quick verdict

A dressier Blundstone with one important sizing caveat

Choose the Blundstone 1306 if you like the lived-in variation of Rustic Brown leather but want a cleaner, more elongated shape than the familiar round-toe 585. The low-profile dress sole, defined welt line and chisel toe make it easier to pair with tailored trousers, dark denim and smart-casual workwear without losing the convenience of a pull-on Chelsea boot.

The buying risk is fit. Blundstone describes the 1306 as longer and wider and explicitly advises some wearers—particularly those with narrow feet—to consider going down half or one full UK size. That guidance is more specific than a generic brand size chart, so do not order automatically from the size you wear in a 585 or 500.

Best for

Smart-casual wardrobes, office-to-weekend use and buyers who prefer a longer chisel-toe silhouette.

Consider first

The Dress last is generous and visually elongated; narrow feet may need a smaller size or added volume control.

Key strengths

  • Distinctive dressier profile
  • Versatile Rustic Brown finish
  • Removable contoured EVA footbed
  • Water-resistant upper

Trade-offs

  • Sizing needs extra care
  • Not fully waterproof
  • Less trail-focused than All-Terrain
  • Rustic finish varies naturally

How does the Blundstone 1306 fit?

Fit is the most important part of this review. Blundstone’s product-specific guidance says the 1306 has a longer, wider fit and recommends considering a half or full size down, especially if your feet are narrow. The Dress Series guide also describes a roomier toe box and an elongated, dressier look. That extra length is partly visual, but it can still translate into excess internal volume when a buyer automatically orders their usual Blundstone size.

Start with the official recommendation rather than assuming all Blundstones fit alike. If you wear a 585, the 1306 may feel longer and less secure at the heel even when both labels show the same UK size. A narrow or low-volume foot is more likely to need the smaller option; a wider foot or high instep may benefit from the Dress last’s room. The correct result should hold the heel without painful pressure across the instep and leave the toes free rather than sliding forward.

Half-size caution: across much of Blundstone’s range, a half size primarily adds width and volume rather than meaningful length. The 1306 page nevertheless tells shoppers to consider half or full-size reductions. Treat that as model-specific starting guidance, then compare both sizes indoors if possible.

Try the boots on carpet late in the day, wearing the socks you will use most often. Walk up and down stairs and check heel movement, flex point and whether the widest part of your foot aligns with the widest part of the boot. A little heel lift can be normal in a pull-on Chelsea; repeated slipping, toe gripping or the foot sliding forward suggests the volume is wrong. Do not rely on thick socks to rescue a fundamentally long fit.

Comfort and break-in

The 1306 combines a leather-and-textile lining with a removable contoured EVA footbed and Blundstone’s SPS shock-protection system. That is a sensible everyday package: the footbed provides shape underfoot, the sole absorbs routine pavement impact and the lining separates the foot from the upper. The official page describes a worn-in feel from the first wear, but that is a manufacturer claim rather than a guarantee of instant comfort for every foot.

Waxed nubuck should soften and crease with use, particularly across the flex point and around the ankle, but break-in cannot correct the wrong length or an unstable heel. Begin with shorter outings and let the upper adapt naturally. Stop if you experience sharp pressure, numbness or persistent rubbing. Heat, soaking and aggressive stretching can damage the leather or adhesives and may affect returns.

The low Dress sole produces a neater profile than the chunkier Classic and All-Terrain options. For commuting, standing, travel and ordinary city days, its balance of cushioning and appearance is the point. People who need specialist arch support should remember that “contoured” is not the same as medically customised; the removable footbed is useful because it may permit a compatible alternative, subject to enough internal volume.

Style: what makes the 1306 different?

The chisel toe is the 1306’s defining feature. Instead of the rounded, workwear-influenced front seen on the 585, the toe extends into a flatter, more angular line. Combined with the slimmer-looking Dress sole, it makes the boot read as smart casual rather than overtly rugged. It can sit naturally under straight or tailored trousers while still working with dark denim and casual layers.

Rustic Brown keeps the result from becoming formal. Waxed nubuck shows light and dark variation, and scuffs become part of the finish rather than disappearing into a uniform polished surface. That makes the 1306 versatile, but not an Oxford substitute for conservative formalwear. If you need a glossy, highly consistent brown shoe for a strict dress code, the rustic texture may be too relaxed.

Colour also varies from photographs. Studio lighting, screen settings and the natural leather surface can make Rustic Brown appear olive, tobacco or dark chocolate. Variation between the two boots is possible. Buyers who want perfectly even colour should choose a smooth-finished model; buyers who like patina are more likely to appreciate why each 1306 looks slightly different over time.

Rustic Brown waxed nubuck: care and ageing

Routine care is simple but material-specific. Let mud dry before using a soft brush, or wipe light dirt with a damp cloth. Allow the boots to air-dry away from radiators, direct heat and strong sunlight. When the upper looks dry, use a non-pigmented product intended for rustic, waxed or oiled nubuck and test it on a hidden area first. Treatment may temporarily darken the leather or reduce contrast in the mottled finish.

Avoid transferring the routine used for smooth full-grain leather without checking the label. Traditional coloured polish can change the nap and produce an uneven shine. Blundstone UK recommends its Rustic Renovating Cream and waterproof spray for this finish; the purpose is conditioning and improved water resistance, not turning the boot into a waterproof membrane design.

Wipe away road salt promptly because it can leave pale lines as moisture evaporates. Remove the footbeds during drying, stuff the boots loosely with plain paper if they are damp, and wait until fully dry before applying care products. Our Blundstone cleaning guide covers the complete method.

Is the Blundstone 1306 waterproof?

No. The 1306 is water-resistant, not waterproof. Conditioned waxed nubuck and the double-stitched upper should handle ordinary wet pavements, splashes and short periods of light rain, but water can eventually enter through the elastic panels, top opening or saturated construction. The low ankle opening also makes deep puddles an obvious limit.

For daily British weather, maintenance and drying discipline matter more than marketing shorthand. Reapply a compatible spray when water stops beading, but do not assume treatment creates the same protection as a sealed waterproof membrane. If regular snow, standing water or prolonged winter exposure is the priority, the Thermal 566 is the more weather-focused Blundstone. See our guide to Blundstone water resistance for the differences.

Is the Blundstone 1306 good for walking?

The 1306 is suitable for ordinary urban walking when it fits securely. Its removable EVA footbed, shock-protection system and pull-on convenience make sense for commuting, travel, shopping and mixed office-to-street use. The elongated toe should not cause a problem when the flex point and heel are correctly aligned.

It is not the strongest choice for technical walking. A Chelsea upper cannot provide the adjustable lockdown of laces, while the Dress sole is less terrain-focused than the Vibram outsole on the 2056 All-Terrain. Mud, steep descents and uneven ground magnify heel movement and demand deeper traction. Choose the 1306 for smarter everyday miles, not as a hiking boot. Blundstone 1306 vs 585 and other alternatives

1306 vs 585

Both use Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, so the decision is mainly shape and platform. For the full side-by-side analysis, see our Blundstone 1306 vs 585 comparison. The 1306 is a Dress Series boot with a longer chisel toe and lower-profile sole. The 585 is a lined Classic with a rounder toe, relaxed upper volume, removable XRD-equipped comfort footbed and more casual workwear character. Choose the 1306 for smart-casual styling; choose the 585 for the familiar rounded Blundstone look.

1306 vs 558

The 558 is a smooth black Classic. It looks clean and versatile but remains rounder and chunkier than the 1306. The 558 makes more sense if you want black, a relaxed Classics fit and adjustable volume through the supplied footbeds. The 1306 is the distinctive option if an elongated silhouette and Rustic Brown finish matter more.

1306 vs 2056

The 2056 also uses Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, but its Vibram outsole and All-Terrain purpose move in the opposite direction. Pick the 2056 for deeper tread and rougher everyday routes. Pick the 1306 for cleaner lines, office compatibility and a dressier sole.

Is the Blundstone 1306 worth £185?

At the official UK price checked on 29 September 2026, value depends on whether you specifically want the Dress silhouette. The 1306 does not justify its price by being more waterproof or trail-capable than cheaper boots. Its proposition is a particular combination: chisel-toe shape, Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, a lined pull-on upper and an everyday shock-absorbing sole.

That combination is compelling if one pair needs to cover commuting, casual offices, dinners and weekends. It is less persuasive if you prefer round toes, need technical grip or struggle to secure a generous pull-on fit. The 585 and 2056 are clearer choices for those priorities. Buy the 1306 because its shape fills a real wardrobe role—not simply because it is a different model number.

Who should buy the Blundstone 1306?

The 1306 is a strong shortlist choice for someone who wants Chelsea-boot convenience with a sharper profile than the standard Blundstone formula. It suits buyers who will use the boot mainly on pavements and indoors, appreciate natural variation in Rustic Brown waxed nubuck and are willing to test sizing carefully.

Skip it if you require guaranteed waterproofing, aggressive trail traction, lace-adjustable heel control or a uniform polished finish. Also pause if returns are difficult: the longer, wider Dress fit creates enough sizing uncertainty that a retailer with straightforward exchanges has real practical value.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Blundstone 1306 run large?

The official product guidance says the 1306 has a longer, wider fit and suggests considering a half or full size down, particularly for narrow feet. Try it indoors with your normal socks because the elongated chisel toe changes how front space looks and feels.

Is the Blundstone 1306 waterproof?

No. Blundstone describes the 1306 as water-resistant rather than waterproof. Maintained waxed nubuck is suitable for ordinary wet pavements and light rain, but not prolonged saturation or deep water.

What is the difference between Blundstone 1306 and 585?

Both use Rustic Brown waxed nubuck, but the 1306 belongs to the Dress Series and has a longer chisel-toe profile with a dress sole. The 585 is a rounder, more casual Classic with a roomier toe area, lining and an XRD-equipped comfort footbed.

Is the Blundstone 1306 unisex?

Yes. Blundstone UK lists the 1306 as a unisex model. The correct choice depends on UK size, foot width, instep and heel fit rather than a men's or women's label.

Is the Blundstone 1306 good for walking?

It is suitable for commuting, travel and ordinary urban walking when the fit is secure. Its dressier sole and pull-on construction are not intended to replace a laced hiking boot on steep, muddy or technical ground.
